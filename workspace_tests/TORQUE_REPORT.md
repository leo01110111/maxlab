# Can these layouts run 24 h continuously without failing an actuator?

**Answer: yes.** Worst case, a joint holds **61 % of the load the datasheet already
warrants the arm can carry continuously** — and that is with the full 7.5 kg rated payload
attached. Unloaded, as the scenes are currently built, it is 38 %.

Generated by `workspace_torque.py`; raw values in `waypoint_torques.csv`.
Specs from the [UR7e technical specification](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/TechSheets/UR7e_techsheet_pdf_online/UR7e_techsheet_en.pdf) (April 2026 revision).

---

## The number that matters

Failure limits are **per joint**. Peak sustained holding torque on the hardest-worked
joint, over the 26 waypoints all three layouts can hold:

| layout | worst joint | unloaded | **+ 7.5 kg rated payload** | % of 150 N·m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `urgantry` | `shoulder_lift` | 35.0 N·m | 70.3 N·m | 47 % |
| `urtable` | `shoulder_lift` | 41.2 N·m | **91.5 N·m** | **61 %** |
| `urtable_45` | `elbow` / `lift` | 56.5 N·m | 69.2 N·m | 46 % |

Wrists stay under 43 % of their smaller ±28 N·m limit in every case (`urtable_45` is the
only layout that loads a wrist meaningfully).

Adding the rated payload roughly doubles the peaks and **reverses the ranking**: unloaded,
`urtable_45` works an actuator hardest; loaded, `urtable` does, because its vertical bases
put everything on `shoulder_lift`.

## Why this is safe, from the datasheet alone

UR does not publish joint torque ratings — the UR7e sheet has none, and its only torque
figures are the tool-flange F/T *sensor* range (±10 N·m). But the payload rating implies
the answer:

> UR7e: **7.5 kg payload, 850 mm reach, any mounting orientation, 0–50 °C ambient.**

A payload rating is a continuous guarantee. Holding 7.5 kg at full reach requires

- payload: 7.5 kg × 9.81 × 0.85 m = **62.5 N·m**
- arm self-weight: 20.6 kg × 9.81 × ~0.35 m ≈ **71 N·m**
- **≈ 133 N·m sustained at the shoulder**

That is the load UR warrants the joint can hold indefinitely, in any orientation, at up to
50 °C ambient. **Our worst measured case is 91.5 N·m — 69 % of it, with the same payload.**
No layout asks the arm for anything it is not already specified to do continuously.

This also corroborates the model's 150 N·m `forcerange`: the arm's own spec implies ~133
N·m of worst-case static demand, so a 150 N·m limit is the right order of magnitude
(89 % of it), which is what a real design would look like.

## Practical note

e-Series joints have mechanical holding brakes that engage when the arm is stopped or
powered down — position held with no motor current and no actuator load. Everything above
assumes 24 h of *active servo-holding*, the worst case. Engaging the brakes for long static
holds removes the question entirely.

## Method

- Both arms driven to each waypoint by IK, tool down, each offset from the waypoint by
  0.35 m along the base-to-base axis; loose props removed from the scene.
- `implicitfast` integrator — with the scenes' default Euler, the stiff position servos
  (kp 2000 / kv 400) chatter at the timestep frequency and no pose reads as settled.
- Physics runs ≥3 s then until `max |q̇| < 1e-3` rad/s (cap 8 s); torques averaged over the
  following 0.25 s. A waypoint counts only if both arms solved IK and settled within 20 mm
  — 33 / 33 / 36 of 45 per layout, 26 in common.
- Payload figures add a 7.5 kg point mass at the TCP analytically (`τ = -Jᵀ·F`) on top of
  the measured holding torque, rather than re-simulating with a loaded gripper.

### Caveats

- **Static holding only.** Motion adds inertial torque this does not capture. The 133 N·m
  datasheet-implied figure is likewise static.
- **150 / 28 N·m is the MuJoCo model's `forcerange`**, inherited from the Menagerie UR5e,
  not a published UR7e rating. The datasheet-derived argument above does not depend on it.
- **The arm CoM distance (~0.35 m) is an estimate**, not a published figure; the 133 N·m
  is therefore approximate. The payload term (62.5 N·m) is exact from datasheet values.
- **26 of 45 waypoints.** Each layout's own-set figures cover different poses.
- Simulation: no friction, gearbox losses, or brake behaviour modelled.
- Earlier versions of this report ranked the layouts differently. Two measurement faults
  (integrator chatter dropping two-thirds of waypoints; a settle threshold loose enough to
  sample torques mid-creep) were found and fixed, and duty-cycle conclusions were
  originally drawn from mean rather than peak torques. The numbers above are from the
  corrected run and reproduce to 0.1 % under an independently written check.
